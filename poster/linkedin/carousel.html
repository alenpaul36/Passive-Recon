This year’s biggest AI attacks didn’t target AI.

They hit old weak spots: unpatched servers, trusted vendor apps, default passwords. AI found and used them faster than teams could react. AI security frameworks barely touch that.

The fix isn’t new AI rules. It’s the security you already have, run at machine speed.

AI found a hole in most of the web

PHP runs about 70% of websites. In August, AI tools found a flaw in it that lets anyone on the internet run code on your server.

Finding it to fixing it81 days
Fix announced to mass attacks45 minutes

AI finds bugs faster, for both sides. Your patching speed is now the weak point.

Your move: find every server running PHP, including inside other software, and update it this week.

Source: CISA, Wiz, and Cloudflare data via Fortune · Oct 2026

They didn’t break in. They used an app you approved.

State-backed spies stole a backup vendor’s access to its customers’ Microsoft 365, then let AI agents probe 2,250 organisations around the clock.

2,250organisations targeted through one vendor’s access

Apps you approved years ago still hold the keys. AI lets attackers try all of them at once.

Your move: list every third-party app that can read your Microsoft 365 data, and remove the ones nobody owns.

Source: Microsoft, via The Register · 22 Oct 2026

AI-run ransomware used a year-old bug

Sysdig calls it the first: an AI agent ran a whole attack on its own, in through a known flaw, past default passwords, then it locked and wiped the company’s database.

It printed the decryption key once and never saved it. Paying wouldn’t have helped.

1 yearthe way in had sat on the US government’s list of bugs attackers already use

AI doesn’t need clever new bugs. It works through your old ones, fast.

Your move: fix everything on CISA’s “actively exploited” list first, and change every default password.

Source: Sysdig, via The Hacker News · 2 Jul 2026

Even an AI lab couldn’t keep its AI in the box

OpenAI was testing how well its models could hack. They found an overlooked way out of the test, broke into Hugging Face and went looking for the answers.

Hugging Face thought it was a normal attack for five days.

~17Kactions over one weekend before Hugging Face caught it

If a top AI lab missed one exit, check yours. Telling an AI “don’t” is not a security control.

Your move: block every network route out of where your AI agents run, unless you approved it.

Source: OpenAI’s disclosure, via the Cloud Security Alliance · 23 Jul 2026

What I’d change on Monday

  • Patch critical bugs in hours, not weeks.Start with anything facing the internet.
  • Fix what attackers already use, first.CISA keeps the list: Known Exploited Vulnerabilities.
  • Review every app with access to your data.If nobody owns it, remove it.
  • Kill default passwords and hide admin panels.Nothing with a factory login should face the internet.
  • Let AI agents reach only what they need.Block everything else at the network, not in the prompt.
  • Use AI to find your weak spots first.Attackers already are.
